## **Overview**

Understanding your **Max Heart Rate (Max HR) and Heart Rate Zones** is key to optimizing your **training, recovery, and performance**. WHOOP personalizes your **HR Zones** using the **Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) method**, which ensures that your training intensity reflects your **current fitness level**. This guide applies to **WHOOP 4.0, 5.0, and MG**, ensuring all members can effectively track and adjust their HR Zones.

## **What is Max Heart Rate?**

**Max Heart Rate (Max HR)** represents the highest number of beats per minute (BPM) your heart can achieve during **maximum exertion**. WHOOP estimates Max HR using **Gellish’s non-linear formula** and adjusts it based on your **recorded peak HR during workouts**.

### **How Max HR Affects Strain**

- Strain is **not directly influenced by HR Zones**, but rather by how much time your heart rate **spends at elevated levels**.

- Strain increases as heart rate moves **closer to Max HR**.


### **How to View Your Max HR in the WHOOP App**

1. Open the **WHOOP app**.

2. Select the **Profile icon**.
   - Toggle to **ALL TIME**

### **How to Adjust Your Max HR**

1. Open the **WHOOP app**.

2. Select **More**.

3. Navigate to **Activity Settings → Heart Rate Settings**.

4. Manually **adjust Max HR** and **Save**.


**Note:** Max HR **naturally decreases over time**. Consider re-evaluating it every **3-5 years**.

* * *

## **Calculating Your Max HR: Testing Methods**

The most accurate method to determine **Max HR** is a **VO2 Max lab test**. However, field tests provide a reliable alternative for experienced athletes.

### **Option 1: 20-Minute Test**

1. Perform a **20-minute run at a hard, sustainable pace**.

2. **Increase effort in the last 3 minutes**.

3. **Sprint all-out for the final 30 seconds**.

4. The highest HR recorded = **Max HR**.


### **Option 2: 4x2 Test**

1. Warm up thoroughly.

2. Perform **4 sets of 2-minute max-effort intervals** with 1-minute rests.

3. Your **highest recorded HR** should approximate your **true Max HR**.


**Note:** These tests should only be performed **when fully recovered** and are **not recommended for beginners**.

* * *

## **What are Heart Rate Zones?**

**HR Zones** categorize effort levels based on your **heart rate relative to Max HR**. WHOOP calculates these zones using the **Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) method**, which factors in both **Max HR and Resting HR (RHR)**.

### **HR Zone Calculation Formula**

**Target HR Intensity Zone = ((Max HR - Resting HR) x %Intensity) + Resting HR**

## **Adjusting HR Zones in the WHOOP App**

WHOOP **automatically updates HR Zones monthly** based on changes to **Resting HR**. However, you can also manually adjust them if needed.

### **How to Adjust HR Zones**

1. Open the **WHOOP app**.

2. Select **More → Activity Settings → Heart Rate Settings**.

3. Toggle **Manual Heart Rate Zones** ON.

4. Adjust **each HR Zone manually** and **Save**.


### **How to Reset HR Zones to Default**

1. Navigate to **HR Settings**.

2. Toggle **Manual Heart Rate Zones OFF**.

3. WHOOP will revert to **auto-adjusted HR Zones**.

## **Viewing HR Zone Trends**

You can track **weekly, monthly, and 6-month trends** in the **WHOOP app**:

- Tap any **metric tile** on the **Home tab** under **My Dashboard**.

- Select **^ and then any HR Zone trend** to analyze time spent ineach HR Zone over time **.**

- Use the **Weekly (W), Monthly (M), or 6-Month (6M) toggles** for deeper insights.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **Why did WHOOP switch to the Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) method?**

- HRR accounts for **both Max HR and Resting HR**, making it a **more precise and personalized approach** that can help you meet your goals than fixed percentage-based zones.


### **Will adjusting my HR Zones affect Strain calculation?**

- No, **Strain is calculated independently** based on time spent at **elevated HR levels relative to Max HR and RHR**.


### **Why does WHOOP only count logged activities toward HR Zone time?**

- Logged activities ensure that HR Zone tracking reflects **intentional training efforts**.

- General movement (e.g., walking, taking stairs) contributes to overall health but is **not included in structured HR Zone data**.


### **How do HR Zones relate to fitness goals?**

- **Zone 1-3:** Build endurance

- **Zone 4-5:** Focus on your athletic performance and power


### **How often should I train in each HR Zone?**

- **Zone 1**: Daily for recovery.

- **Zone 2**: 2-3x per week for aerobic base.

- **Zone 3**: 1-2x per week for endurance.

- **Zone 4**: 1-2x per week for performance gains.

- **Zone 5**: Sparingly (~1x per week for peak efforts).


### **What if my HR Zones feel inaccurate?**

- Ensure **WHOOP fits snugly** on your wrist or body.

- Check that your **Max HR and Resting HR are correct**.

- HR Zones **adjust automatically** based on fitness level changes.
